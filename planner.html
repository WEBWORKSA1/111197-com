    How the generator decides your numbers

    The plan divides the gap between your target and what you’ve already saved by the number of whole months until your deadline. Weekly and daily figures are the same amount expressed at a finer scale (×12÷52 and ×12÷365), which makes small goals feel achievable and big goals honest.

    The realism check compares the monthly amount with your take-home pay. Up to 10% is comfortable, 10–20% matches the savings share most budgeting frameworks recommend, 20–35% is a stretch that usually needs both a spending cut and an income boost, and anything above that is a sign to extend the deadline or lower the target. These are rules of thumb, not guarantees.

    Why the starter cushion and the 11% debt rule come before the goal

    Plans usually fail on a surprise expense, not on motivation. A small cushion — we suggest the lower of 10% of your goal or $1,111 — stops one car repair from wiping out months of progress. Likewise, debt charging more than about 11% a year is costing you more than most savings accounts or conservative investments earn, so paying it down in parallel is often the fastest way to “save.”
