The $1,111 starter cushion

The most important money goal is the boring one: cash that turns an emergency into an inconvenience.

111197 editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 6-minute read

The two-stage approach

  • Stage 1: a starter cushion of about $1,111 (or one month of essentials if that’s lower).
  • Stage 2: a full fund of 3–6 months of essential expenses — longer if your income is irregular.

Why a cushion comes first

Without one, every surprise — a phone screen, a dental bill, a late paycheck — goes on a credit card at 20%+ interest, and your other goals stall. A cushion breaks that cycle. It doesn’t need to be large to work; it needs to exist and be untouchable for anything except emergencies.

How big should the full fund be?

Add up essentials only: housing, utilities, groceries, transport, insurance and minimum debt payments. Multiply by:

  • 3 months — stable salaried job, no dependants, low fixed costs.
  • 6 months — single income household, dependants, or a specialised job that takes longer to replace.
  • 6–12 months — freelancers, gig workers, commission earners and business owners.

Example: $2,080 of monthly essentials × 3 = $6,240. The emergency fund calculator does this for you and shows an 11-month path.

If $567 a month is unrealistic, stretch the timeline — but keep the starter cushion fast.

Where to keep it

  • A separate high-interest savings account, ideally at a different bank from your everyday account.
  • Covered by your country’s deposit-insurance scheme (e.g. FDIC/NCUA in the US, CDIC in Canada, FSCS in the UK).
  • Accessible within a day or two — not invested in stocks or crypto, which can fall exactly when you need cash.

What counts as an emergency?

Use a three-question test: Is it necessary? Is it urgent? Is it unexpected? A job loss, medical bill or essential car repair passes. Concert tickets and sales don’t — those belong in your wants or goal budget.
