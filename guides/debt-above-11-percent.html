Kill debt above 11%

High-interest debt is a goal-eater. Every month it’s alive, part of your income goes to the lender instead of your future.

111197 editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 7-minute read

Key takeaways

  • Paying off a 22.9% card is like earning a guaranteed 22.9% — far above typical savings or long-run market returns.
  • On a $4,000 balance at 22.9%, raising the payment from $150 to $200 saves about $556 and a full year.
  • Choose avalanche for the lowest cost, snowball for the fastest motivation — both beat minimums.

Why 11%?

There’s nothing magic about 11 — it’s a practical line. Long-run stock market averages are often quoted in the high single digits, before inflation and with real risk; savings accounts earn less. Debt charging more than about 11% almost always costs more than your money can safely earn, so paying it down is the better “investment.” Lower-rate debt (many student loans, mortgages) can usually be paid on schedule while you build savings.

The minimum-payment trap

Here’s a $4,000 card balance at 22.9% APR:

Monthly paymentTime to pay offTotal interest
$11162 months (5y 2m)≈ $2,834
$15038 months (3y 2m)≈ $1,643
$20026 months (2y 2m)≈ $1,087

Paying $89 more a month (from $111 to $200) cuts the interest by about $1,750 and three years. Test your own balance with the debt payoff calculator.

Avalanche vs snowball

Both methods pay the minimum on every debt and send all extra money to one target:

  • Avalanche — target the highest interest rate first. Mathematically cheapest.
  • Snowball — target the smallest balance first. Quick wins build momentum, which research on motivation suggests helps many people stick with it.

Our default: avalanche for anything above 11%, but if you have a tiny balance you can clear in a month or two, knock it out first for the win.

Five ways to speed it up

  1. Call your card issuer and ask for a lower rate — it works more often than people expect, especially with on-time history.
  2. Consider a balance transfer or consolidation loan only if the fees and new rate are clearly lower and you stop adding new charges.
  3. Round payments up and pay every two weeks to cut interest.
  4. Send windfalls (refunds, bonuses, gifts) straight to the target debt.
  5. Add an income lever — see AI side income or negotiating a raise.

When to get help

If minimums are more than you can pay, contact a non-profit credit counselling agency in your country before missing payments; they can negotiate repayment plans. Be wary of anyone charging large upfront fees or promising to “erase” debt. Our free plan form can request a vetted introduction.
