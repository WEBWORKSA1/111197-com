11.11 without the debt

The world’s biggest shopping season is engineered to make you buy. Here’s how to make it work for your goals instead.

What 11.11 has become

What started as China’s informal “Singles’ Day” on 11 November grew into the largest online shopping event in the world. It now runs for weeks — the 2025 campaign started in early October on major platforms — and has spread to many other countries alongside Black Friday and Cyber Monday. Platforms stopped publishing total sales several years ago, but third-party estimates for recent festivals run well over a trillion yuan. Longer campaigns mean more “limited-time” pressure spread over more days.

The five-rule playbook

1. Write the list before you see the ads

List what you actually need or have been planning to buy for months. Anything not on the list must survive rule 4.

2. Set a hard cap — from your wants budget

Decide a total in advance, funded only from your wants money or a sinking fund you’ve saved for this. Never from your emergency fund or goal account.

3. Check price history

“Original” prices are sometimes inflated before a sale. Use price-history tools or screenshots from a few weeks earlier. A real deal is a discount on a price people actually paid.

4. The 11-hour rule

For any unplanned item, add it to your cart and wait 11 hours. If you still want it and it fits the cap, buy it. Most impulse urges fade long before.

5. Skip buy-now-pay-later for wants

Splitting a purchase into instalments makes it feel smaller, but it stacks quietly with other plans and late fees. If you can’t pay for a want today, it isn’t in this year’s budget.

Turn the savings into a goal

If something you planned to buy anyway is 30% off, the discount is real money. Move the amount you saved — the difference — straight into your goal account the same day. That turns sale season into a contribution instead of a leak.

A note on 11 November

The same date is Remembrance Day and Veterans Day in many countries, marking the 1918 Armistice. Many people pause for two minutes of silence at 11 a.m. — a reasonable moment to put the phone down before checkout, too.
