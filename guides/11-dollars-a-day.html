What $11.11 a day becomes

The price of a lunch, invested every day, compounds into something serious. Here’s the math — and the catch.

111197 editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 6-minute read

Key numbers

  • $11.11 a day ≈ $337.93 a month ≈ $4,055 a year.
  • At 7% a year for 30 years: about $412,000, of which you contributed about $122,000.
  • Starting 10 years later roughly halves the result.

Why time matters more than the amount

Look at the jump from 30 to 40 years at 7%: you add about $40,000 of deposits but the balance grows by roughly $475,000. That’s compounding — growth on top of previous growth. It’s also why someone born in 1997 who starts at 29 has a large advantage over the same person starting at 39, even with identical deposits. The rule of 72 video explains a quick mental shortcut: at 7%, money roughly doubles every 10 years.

Where $11.11 a day can come from

  • Subscriptions: two or three unused streaming or app subscriptions often total $30–$50 a month.
  • Food delivery: replacing two delivery orders a week with home meals can free $150+ a month.
  • A raise: a 3% raise on $48,000 is about $120 a month after tax — more than a third of the target.
  • A small side service: 4–5 hours a month at $25/hour covers the rest. Try the side hustle hours calculator.

Where to put it

Order matters. Most educators suggest: first a starter cushion, then paying off high-interest debt, then long-term investing in diversified, low-cost funds — often inside a tax-advantaged account available in your country (for example a 401(k) or IRA in the US, a TFSA or RRSP in Canada, an ISA in the UK, or PPF/NPS in India). Money you’ll need within a few years belongs in savings, not the stock market.

The catch

Returns are never guaranteed, inflation reduces what future dollars buy, and fees quietly compound against you. A 1% annual fee on a 30-year plan can cost a five-figure sum. Keep costs low, stay diversified, and don’t stop contributing during down years — that’s when the same deposit buys more.
