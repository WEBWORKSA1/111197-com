The 11-step money plan

A simple, ordered system for turning any wish — a safety net, a trip, debt freedom, a business — into a goal that actually gets funded.

111197 editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 8-minute read

In short

  • Steps 1–5 make the goal concrete: words, number, date, gap, split.
  • Steps 6–8 protect it: a cushion, high-interest debt first, automation.
  • Steps 9–11 speed it up and keep it alive: an income lever, a monthly review, a reset.

Most money goals don’t fail because people are lazy. They fail because the goal was never specific enough to schedule, or because one surprise bill knocked it off course. The 11 Steps fix both problems in a fixed order. We built the framework around the number 11 deliberately: if you’re someone who notices 11:11 on the clock, you now have a built-in reminder.

Steps 1–5: make it concrete

01 · Name the wish

Write one sentence you could read aloud: “Build a $5,000 emergency fund,” not “be better with money.” A named goal is easier to say no on behalf of — when a tempting purchase shows up, you know exactly what it competes with.

02 · Put a number on it

Price the goal properly. For a trip, add flights, stays, food and a 10% buffer. For a move, include deposit, first month’s rent, movers and basics. For debt, use today’s balance. Unpriced wishes can’t be funded.

03 · Set the date

A deadline converts hope into a schedule. If you have no external date, use a default of 11 months: long enough to be realistic, short enough to stay motivating.

04 · Find the gap

Target minus what you already have. This is the only number that matters for planning — and it’s often smaller than the goal felt.

05 · Split it

Divide the gap by the months until your deadline, then express it per week and per day. A $4,500 gap over 11 months is about $409 a month, $94 a week or $13.45 a day. Daily numbers make trade-offs obvious.

Steps 6–8: protect it

06 · Build a starter cushion

Before chasing the goal, set aside a small buffer — we suggest the lower of 10% of the goal or $1,111. Its only job is to absorb the flat tyre or vet bill that would otherwise raid your goal or land on a credit card. See the $1,111 starter cushion.

07 · Kill debt above 11%

Any debt charging more than roughly 11% a year usually costs more than your savings can earn. Paying it down is a guaranteed “return” at that rate. Keep making minimums on everything, and push extra at the highest rate. See debt above 11%.

08 · Automate on payday

Schedule the monthly amount to move to a separate, named account on the day you’re paid. Saving first and spending what’s left beats trying to save “whatever’s left” at month-end.

Steps 9–11: accelerate and sustain

09 · Add an income lever

Cutting costs has a floor; earning more doesn’t. A raise, a few overtime shifts, freelancing or an AI-assisted side service can shorten the timeline dramatically. Even 25% more than your monthly target turns 11 months into about 9.

10 · Run the 11th Review

On the 11th of every month, spend 11 minutes: check your balance against the plan, adjust the amount if life changed, and note one win. Missing a month isn’t failure; skipping the review is how plans quietly die.

11 · Celebrate & reset

When you hit the number, celebrate — within a budget you set in advance. Then decide what the automatic transfer funds next. The habit is the real asset.

Common questions

Should I invest instead of saving for a short goal? For money you need within about three years, most educators favour stable options like high-interest savings, because markets can fall right when you need the cash.

What if the monthly amount is too high? You have three levers: extend the date, lower the target, or add income. The plan’s realism check tells you which is most urgent.
