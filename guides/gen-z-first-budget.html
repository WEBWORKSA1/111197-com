Budgeting

Your first real budget

A first paycheck feels huge until rent, transit and a friend’s birthday dinner arrive in the same week. Here’s a budget designed for real life.

111197 editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 7-minute read

The setup in one line

  • Three accounts, one payday transfer, one monthly review — and percentages you adjust to your city.

Why young adults feel squeezed

You’re not imagining it. Bank of America’s 2026 Better Money Habits study of adults aged 18–29 found that 42% describe themselves as living paycheck to paycheck, even as about two-thirds are actively saving. Housing and everyday costs are the main pressure. The good news from the same research: young adults are increasingly open about money with friends — and that openness is a budgeting superpower.

Step 1: start with take-home pay

Budget from what lands in your account after tax and deductions, not your salary. If your income varies (gig work, tips, commissions), use the average of your three lowest recent months.

Step 2: the 50/30/20 starting split

In expensive cities, needs often take 60% or more. That’s fine as a starting point — shift from wants, not from future you, where you can. Try different splits in the 50/30/20 calculator.

Step 3: the three-account setup

  1. Bills account — needs only. Rent and fixed bills come out automatically.
  2. Spending account — wants. When it’s empty, the month’s fun is done. No guilt, no spreadsheets.
  3. Goal account — future you, at a separate bank or in a high-interest savings account so it isn’t visible in your everyday app.

On payday, one automatic split sends money to all three. You never “decide” to save; it simply happens.

Step 4: loud budgeting

Saying “that’s not in my budget this month — can we do a picnic instead?” is now normal. Research on young adults shows many already discuss money openly with friends. Being loud about your goal recruits allies instead of creating awkward moments.

Step 5: plan for irregular costs

Birthdays, annual subscriptions, car registration and holidays are predictable — just not monthly. List them, total them, divide by 12 and add that amount to your needs bucket.

Step 6: the 11th Review

On the 11th of each month, take 11 minutes: compare spending to plan, move any leftover wants money to your goal, and adjust next month’s numbers. Small corrections beat big resets.
